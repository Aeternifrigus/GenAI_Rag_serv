# Severity Definitions

Severity one means total unavailability of a customer-facing service, or
confirmed loss or exposure of customer data. Any single customer being unable to
work does not qualify unless the cause is systemic.

Severity two means a customer-facing service is degraded but usable, or a
critical internal system is fully unavailable. Elevated error rates above five
percent sustained for ten minutes fall here.

Severity three means a defect with a workaround, a single-tenant problem, or a
non-critical internal system being unavailable. Most reported issues land here.

Severity four is cosmetic or documentation-only, tracked as normal backlog work
rather than as an incident.

Severity is assigned by the first responder and may be raised or lowered at any
point during the incident. Lowering severity requires agreement from the
incident commander. Raising it requires no agreement and may be done by anyone.

Severity drives the response clock, the communication obligations and whether a
postmortem is mandatory. It does not drive priority of the eventual fix, which
is set separately during triage.
